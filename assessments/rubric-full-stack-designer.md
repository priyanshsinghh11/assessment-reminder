## What this assessment tests  
A strong hire must demonstrate the ability to **quickly produce high‑impact visual and interaction design** that lifts a live website, crafts a realistic, trust‑focused product flow for a regulated (HIPAA) environment, and creates on‑brand, executive‑level communication assets—all while leveraging AI tools efficiently and explaining their contribution.

---

## What each category means here  

- **Problem understanding** – The candidate identifies the exact deliverables (website page redesign, healthcare‑translator UI flow, LinkedIn post + graphic, AI‑workflow note, walkthrough video) and the constraints (4‑hour limit, conversion focus, compliance, brand tone, AI‑native workflow). Markers look for a clear statement of the chosen page, the chosen user scenario, and why each artifact was prioritized.  

- **Depth of reasoning** – The answer goes beyond “here’s a design” and explains *why* design decisions were made: hierarchy choices, motion rationale, trust signals for HIPAA, conversion levers, and AI tool selection. Trade‑offs (e.g., speed vs. fidelity, visual flair vs. compliance) are named and justified.  

- **Domain craft** – Shows senior‑level full‑stack design skill:  
  - **Website** – production‑ready layout, responsive breakpoints, motion spec (easing, trigger, duration), visual system that aligns with Ajaia’s brand palette and typography.  
  - **Product UI** – clear information architecture, state diagrams (loading, error, secure), accessible controls, compliance‑focused microcopy, realistic workflow steps.  
  - **LinkedIn asset** – on‑brand graphic dimensions, hierarchy that drives a single takeaway, copy that reads like a senior Ajaia thought‑leader.  
  - **AI workflow** – specific tools (e.g., Midjourney for imagery, Claude for copy, Figma AI for component generation) and a clear split between AI‑generated output and human refinement.  

- **Practical execution** – The submission includes the actual files or exports, annotated mock‑ups, a concise animation description, a step‑by‑step user journey, a copy‑ready LinkedIn post, a working YouTube link, and a brief AI‑usage note. Anything that would let an engineer or marketer pick up the work immediately scores high.  

- **Communication** – The write‑up is well‑structured (intro → rationale → each deliverable → AI note → video summary), uses headings or bullet points, and keeps explanations concise while still providing enough context for a reviewer to follow the thought process.

## What a strong answer does  

- **Selects a high‑traffic page** (e.g., Home or Pricing) and explains why that page most impacts conversion.  
- **Provides side‑by‑side before/after** mock‑ups with clearly labeled responsive breakpoints (desktop, tablet, mobile).  
- **Specifies motion details** (e.g., “hero fade‑in 300 ms, ease‑out‑cubic; button hover scale 1.05, 150 ms”) and links them to the brand’s tone.  
- **Maps a realistic healthcare workflow** (e.g., patient check‑in → language detection → live transcription screen → review/export) with at least three key screens and annotated states (loading, error, secure lock icon).  
- **Includes compliance cues** (HIPAA badge, data‑encryption notice, consent toggle) and explains their placement.  
- **Delivers a LinkedIn post** that follows Ajaia’s voice, contains a headline, a 2‑sentence insight from the 2026 report, a clear CTA, and a graphic sized to LinkedIn’s recommended 1200 × 628 px with brand colors and a data‑visual element.  
- **Lists AI tools used** (e.g., “Midjourney for hero illustration, Claude for copy brainstorming, Figma’s AI‑layout for component generation”) and notes which decisions were manually refined.  
- **Shares an unlisted YouTube walkthrough** (≤ 3 min) that walks through time allocation, design rationale, animation approach, and AI workflow, with timestamps in the video description.  

---

## What a weak answer does  

- **Chooses a low‑impact page** without justification or provides only a static screenshot.  
- **Omits responsive or motion details**, or gives vague statements like “looks good on mobile.”  
- **Presents a UI flow that is generic** (e.g., a simple chat screen) with no healthcare‑specific constraints or state handling.  
- **Leaves out compliance elements** (no HIPAA references, no security cues).  
- **Provides a LinkedIn copy that is off‑brand or a graphic that is low‑resolution/incorrect dimensions.**  
- **Mentions AI tools only in passing** or fails to explain what was AI‑generated vs. human‑crafted.  
- **No video walkthrough or a video that merely shows the screens without explanation.**
