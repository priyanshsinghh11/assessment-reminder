## What this assessment tests  
A strong hire must demonstrate the ability to **pick the right Ajaia product for an early go‑to‑market push**, articulate a credible first‑customer profile, and design a concrete 2‑3‑week launch experiment.  They must also produce **sharp, founder‑level narrative assets** and show an **AI‑native workflow that adds real leverage** while keeping strategic judgment front‑and‑center.

## What each category means here  

- **Problem understanding** – The answer directly addresses the five tasks (product choice, target customer, launch plan, narrative assets, reasoning & AI note). The candidate recognises the real constraints: limited time, public data only, need for a decision‑grade memo, and the requirement to be “AI‑native.”  
- **Depth of reasoning** – The response goes beyond “it looks good.” It analyses market signals, product‑fit trade‑offs, hypothesis framing, metric selection, and the rationale for the chosen narrative angle. Trade‑offs are named, alternatives are weighed, and conclusions are backed by evidence or logical inference.  
- **Domain craft** – Shows founder‑office skill set: strategic product‑launch judgment, concise market segmentation, experiment design (hypothesis → channels → metrics), high‑impact writing (LinkedIn post, case‑study, outbound message) that matches Ajaia’s voice, and disciplined AI‑tool usage (prompt design, output editing, citation of sources).  
- **Practical execution** – Delivers **actionable** artifacts: a memo with bullet‑pointed plan, a launch timeline with dates/owners, concrete channel tactics, specific metric thresholds, and ready‑to‑send copy for each narrative asset. The AI workflow note lists exact tools, prompts, and what the candidate edited themselves.  
- **Communication** – The whole submission is well‑structured (clear headings, logical flow), concise, and free of jargon. Each deliverable reads like a finished piece that could be handed to the founder today.

## What a strong answer does  

1. **Selects ONE Ajaia product** and states the choice in the first sentence, citing two‑three concrete product features and one market trend that give it the highest short‑term launch upside.  
2. **Explains the problem the product solves** with a brief, data‑backed description of the pain point (e.g., “reducing manual data‑reconciliation time for mid‑size fintechs by 40 %”).  
3. **Defines a first‑customer segment** with four exact attributes: industry, company size (e.g., “$50‑$200 M ARR”), buyer role (e.g., “Head of Operations”), and a justification linking the segment to the product’s strongest value proposition.  
4. **Presents a 2‑3‑week launch experiment** that includes:  
   - a single, testable hypothesis (e.g., “If we position X as a cost‑saving tool, 15 % of outreach emails will book a demo”),  
   - the positioning angle (one‑sentence tagline),  
   - a channel mix with specific tactics (LinkedIn Sponsored Content, targeted email sequence, founder‑hosted webinar),  
   - a day‑by‑day action list with owners,  
   - three key metrics (e.g., email open rate, demo‑booking conversion, pilot‑sign‑up), and  
   - a clear success/failure rule (e.g., “≥ 10 demo bookings = success”).  
5. **Delivers three narrative assets** that are ready to publish: a LinkedIn post (≤ 150 words, includes hook, value claim, CTA), a case‑study style paragraph (problem → solution → quantified impact), and an outbound message (subject line + 3‑sentence body). All copy mirrors Ajaia’s tone and includes a concrete hook for the target buyer.  
6. **Provides a concise Reasoning & AI Workflow note** that:  
   - lists every AI tool used (e.g., ChatGPT‑4 for first‑draft, Claude for tone‑tuning, Midjourney for mock‑up),  
   - shows one example prompt,  
   - explains what the candidate edited or added, and  
   - outlines the key assumptions (e.g., “Assume product X integrates with Salesforce”) and the biggest trade‑off considered (e.g., “speed to market vs. depth of integration”).  

## What a weak answer does  

1. Picks a product but gives only a vague “it looks cool” reason with no market or feature justification.  
2. Describes a target customer in broad terms (e.g., “tech companies”) without size, role, or why they’re the best fit.  
3. Supplies a launch plan that is high‑level (“run ads”) with no hypothesis, no metric thresholds, and no timeline or owners.  
4. Provides narrative assets that are generic marketing copy, missing Ajaia‑specific language, or are unfinished drafts.  
5. Omits the AI workflow note, or lists tools without showing how they influenced the output.  
6. Fails to discuss any assumptions or trade‑offs, making the reasoning appear ungrounded.  

## Score bands  

- **90‑100 %** – Meets or exceeds every rubric item; the memo is decision‑grade, the launch plan is fully actionable, all three narrative assets are polished and on‑brand, and the AI workflow is transparent and adds clear value.  
- **75‑89 %** – Strong overall answer; minor gaps such as a slightly less detailed metric set, a modestly generic line in one narrative asset, or a brief AI note, but still demonstrates solid judgment and execution.  
- **60‑74 %** – Satisfies the basic requirements; product choice and customer segment are clear, but the launch plan lacks depth (e.g., missing owners or success criteria) or one of the narrative assets is under‑developed. AI usage is mentioned but not well‑explained.  
- **40‑59 %** – Partial fulfillment; major elements are missing or superficial (e.g., no hypothesis, no metrics, or only one narrative asset delivered). Reasoning shows limited analysis and AI workflow is absent or token.  
- **0‑39 %** – Fails to address the core tasks; either no product is selected, the answer is off‑topic, or the submission is a vague essay with no concrete deliverables, metrics, or evidence of AI‑native work.
