## What this assessment tests  
A strong hire must demonstrate that they can **run end‑to‑end recruiting as a repeatable system**, juggle internal and client‑facing demands, and **leverage AI tools to accelerate sourcing, screening, and pipeline health**. They also need to show **judgment in prioritizing speed vs quality**, clear stakeholder communication, and the ability to design and execute a scalable recruiting operation from day 1.

---

## What each category means here  

- **Problem understanding** – The answer directly addresses the three‑simultaneous incident items, the full system design brief, and the AI‑usage requirement. Markers look for evidence that the candidate has parsed the constraints (tight 10‑day deadline, inconsistent feedback, candidate drop‑off) and has scoped the deliverables (architecture, workflows, client model, evaluation rubric, metrics, 30‑day plan, AI tool list, video).  

- **Depth of reasoning** – The response goes beyond “we’ll do X”. It explains *why* a step is chosen, weighs trade‑offs (speed vs bias, automation vs human judgment), cites data points or industry benchmarks, and connects root‑cause analysis to the recovery plan.  

- **Domain craft** – Shows recruiting expertise at a senior‑manager level: knowledge of ATS structures, stage definitions, sourcing Boolean tactics, interview scorecards, metric formulas, and client‑service delivery models. Concrete references to tools (e.g., Greenhouse, Lever, Hiretual, Lattice, Zapier, OpenAI embeddings) and proven recruiting practices are required.  

- **Practical execution** – Provides actionable items: exact communication templates, a 3‑day sourcing sprint cadence, a weekly metric dashboard layout, a step‑by‑step AI workflow (prompt, model, output handling). The plan must be implementable without further clarification.  

- **Communication** – The answer is organized with clear headings, bullet‑pointed steps, short paragraphs, and any diagrams are labeled. It avoids vague prose and uses consistent terminology throughout.

## What a strong answer does  

- **Identifies the three incident owners** (client request, hiring‑manager complaint, candidate withdrawal) and assigns a 24‑hour owner for each.  
- **Drafts a concise 24‑hour communication script** for the client (acknowledgement + provisional timeline) and for the hiring manager (request for calibrated feedback).  
- **Pins the root cause to specific stages** (e.g., sourcing breadth, scorecard mis‑alignment, feedback latency) and cites evidence (e.g., “average feedback time = 4 days vs SLA 1 day”).  
- **Outlines a rapid‑pipeline rebuild**: AI‑augmented sourcing (e.g., Claude‑based Boolean expansion + LinkedIn Recruiter), parallel screening pods, and a “fast‑track” interview sprint with 48‑hour decision windows.  
- **Presents a full ATS schema** (e.g., “Intake → Sourcing → AI‑Screen → Human Screen → Interview Loop → Offer”) with separate “Internal” and “Client” pipelines and a shared “Candidate Pool” table.  
- **Describes three concrete AI workflows**, each with: tool name, prompt/template, automation trigger (Zapier/Webhook), human‑in‑the‑loop checkpoint, and KPI impact (e.g., “reduce sourcing time 30 %”).  
- **Creates a client‑delivery model** that converts a client brief into a hiring scorecard, defines weekly status cadence, and sets SLA expectations (e.g., 10‑day delivery for urgent roles).  
- **Builds a candidate evaluation rubric** that scores technical, product, and cultural fit on a 1‑5 scale, includes calibrated interview guides, and explains how AI‑generated resume summaries are used for consistency but never replace human judgment.  
- **Lists weekly metrics** (time‑to‑fill, offer‑acceptance, feedback‑turnaround, AI‑source conversion %, pipeline health score) and shows a simple dashboard mock‑up.  
- **Provides a 30‑day execution timeline** with week‑by‑week deliverables, quick‑win automations (e.g., AI‑drafted outreach), and prioritization logic for the three urgent AI Engineer roles.  
- **Specifies AI tools** (ChatGPT for outreach copy, Claude for Boolean generation, Vector DB for resume embeddings, LLM‑based sentiment analysis for feedback) and explicitly notes where human judgment remains mandatory (final interview debrief, bias checks).  
- **Includes a link to a 5‑8‑minute video** with the required title/description, and the video walks through the system diagram, incident response, and AI workflow trade‑offs.

## What a weak answer does  

- **Leaves one or more incident items unaddressed** (e.g., mentions the client request but ignores the hiring‑manager complaint).  
- **Provides only high‑level statements** (“we’ll use AI to source”) without naming tools, prompts, or hand‑off points.  
- **Omits a root‑cause analysis** or offers a generic “lack of communication” without linking it to a specific stage.  
- **Fails to separate internal vs client pipelines** or presents a single, vague ATS layout.  
- **Describes AI usage in abstract terms** (“AI will make everything faster”) and does not list concrete workflows or expected metric improvements.  
- **Does not include a measurable 30‑day plan** – missing weekly milestones, priorities, or automation targets.  
- **Lacks a candidate evaluation rubric** or provides a rubric that cannot be scored (e.g., only “good/average/poor” without criteria).  
- **Missing the required video link** or provides a link that does not meet the naming convention.
