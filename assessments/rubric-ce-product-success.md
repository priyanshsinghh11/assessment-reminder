## What this assessment tests  
A strong hire must demonstrate the ability to **design and communicate a concise, phased rollout plan for a large, multi‑stakeholder education district**, and to **prioritize and resolve simultaneous support issues with clear, non‑technical language and a rigorous internal bug report**.  The candidate also needs to show they can **diagnose systemic CX problems from limited metrics and propose concrete, scalable improvements, including realistic AI use‑cases**.

---

## What each category means here  

- **Problem understanding** – The answer directly addresses every prompt (30‑day onboarding table, ticket triage, full handling of Ticket C, top CX challenge, 90‑day plan, AI opportunities).  Markers look for evidence that the candidate recognized the district’s size, stakeholder mix, and the need to compress the plan to one page.  

- **Depth of reasoning** – The response explains *why* a particular phase, metric, or priority was chosen, weighs trade‑offs (e.g., speed vs. training depth), and justifies escalation decisions.  It should reference constraints such as limited support bandwidth or risk of data duplication.  

- **Domain craft** – Shows concrete knowledge of **K‑12 SaaS rollout, permission models, roster sync, AI‑generated content, and support operations**.  Expect specific terminology (e.g., “role‑based access control”, “batch roster import”, “CSAT”, “first‑response SLA”) and realistic tools or processes (e.g., “weekly stakeholder sync via Teams”, “Jira bug ticket”, “knowledge‑base article template”).  

- **Practical execution** – Provides actionable artifacts: a markdown table with weeks, milestones, owners, cadence; a 2‑sentence customer email; a structured engineering bug report with reproducible steps; measurable metrics; and concrete initiative steps (e.g., “publish self‑service password‑reset guide”).  

- **Communication** – Information is organized, concise, and easy to scan.  Tables, headings, and bullet lists are used where appropriate, and language for teachers/administrators is jargon‑free.  The video brief is not graded here, but written clarity matters.

## What a strong answer does  

- Presents a **markdown table** that splits the 30‑day plan into **Week 1, Weeks 2‑3, Week 4**, each with a single, clearly‑named milestone.  
- Assigns **ownership** for every major task (e.g., “District IT – network whitelist”, “You – kickoff training”).  
- Specifies a **communication cadence** (e.g., “daily stand‑up email, weekly stakeholder call”).  
- Lists **two quantitative success metrics** (e.g., “% of teachers who complete onboarding training”, “average time to first successful AI lesson plan”).  
- Identifies **one top rollout risk** (e.g., “permissions mis‑configuration”) and outlines a proactive mitigation (e.g., “pre‑flight permission audit”).  
- Ranks the three tickets **A → B → C** (or the correct order) with **2‑3 sentences** each, noting urgency, impact, and whether escalation is required.  
- Supplies a **customer‑friendly reply** to Ticket C ≤ 150 words, using plain language and next steps.  
- Delivers a **complete internal bug report** for Ticket C with all required fields, including a realistic severity rating and a mock attachment description (e.g., “log snippet, screenshot”).  
- Pinpoints a **single CX challenge** from the metric list (e.g., “high password‑reset volume”) and explains why fixing it will improve CSAT and support load.  
- Proposes **three 90‑day initiatives**, each limited to 3‑4 sentences, linking the initiative to adoption, satisfaction, or efficiency.  
- Suggests **two concrete AI use‑cases** (e.g., “AI‑generated FAQ draft”, “auto‑categorization of support tickets”) with a brief impact statement and one realistic limitation.  

---

## What a weak answer does  

- Omits the markdown table or provides a free‑form paragraph instead of a clear phased plan.  
- Leaves ownership or cadence vague (“the team will handle it”) and does not name success metrics.  
- Ranks tickets without justification, or mixes up the order, and fails to indicate escalation.  
- Supplies only a generic “We are looking into it” reply for Ticket C and no internal bug report.  
- Lists many challenges or initiatives without prioritizing or linking them to the data provided.  
- Uses technical jargon in the customer email or provides overly long, unfocused explanations.  
- Misses the required word limits, making the response too verbose or incomplete.

## Score bands  

- **90‑100** – Meets every bullet in “What a strong answer does”; reasoning is deep, domain‑specific, and all artifacts are precise, concise, and well‑structured.  
- **75‑89** – Covers most strong‑answer bullets; minor omissions (e.g., one metric missing or a slightly weaker justification) but overall solid execution.  
- **60‑74** – Satisfies basic requirements (plan, triage, bug report) but lacks depth, some metrics, or clear risk mitigation; communication is adequate but not polished.  
- **40‑59** – Delivers many components but with significant gaps (e.g., no ownership matrix, weak triage reasoning, missing AI suggestions) or major clarity issues.  
- **0‑39** – Fails to address key prompts, provides incorrect or incoherent artifacts, or shows little understanding of the role’s domain.
